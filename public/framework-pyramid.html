How I Think — Model 04

Pyramid Principle

Answer first, structure second — how I write every recommendation, memo, and executive deck.

What It Is

The Pyramid Principle structures communication by leading with the answer or recommendation, then supporting it with grouped, structured reasons — rather than building up to a conclusion through narrative. The reader gets the takeaway in the first line, whether or not they read the rest.

Why It Matters

Executives read the first line of a deck or memo and decide whether to keep reading. Burying the recommendation on slide 12 loses the room before the argument is even made — the structure has to survive someone reading only the headline.

How I Apply It

  1. State the recommendation or conclusion in the first line.
  2. Group supporting arguments into three or four clearly labelled reasons.
  3. Support each reason with evidence, not narrative build-up.
  4. Cut anything that doesn't directly support the stated conclusion.

In Practice — Executive Communications

Translating complex organizational updates into leadership narratives for CEO and client engagements meant every deck had to work even if the executive only read the title slide. I structured presentations and business reviews to lead with the takeaway and then support it — never building up to a reveal the room doesn't have time for.

Result → Executive-ready presentations for leadership forums, client engagements, and annual business reviews
