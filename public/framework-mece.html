How I Think — Model 02

MECE Structuring

Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive — the discipline behind every framework I build.

What It Is

MECE structuring means breaking a problem into parts that don't overlap (mutually exclusive) and that together cover the whole picture (collectively exhaustive). It's the discipline consultants use to make sure a recommendation is neither double-counted nor missing a real scenario.

Why It Matters

Without MECE, strategy documents either double-count the same idea under two different names, or leave a real gap unaddressed — both erode a leadership team's trust in the recommendation the moment someone pokes at the structure.

How I Apply It

  1. List every factor that could plausibly matter to the problem.
  2. Test each pair for overlap — if two items could both be true at once, they aren't mutually exclusive.
  3. Test the full set for gaps — could a real scenario fall outside every category listed?
  4. Collapse, split, or rename categories until the structure holds under both tests.

In Practice — The Computacenter EVP Framework

Employer branding conversations often collapse into one vague pillar — ‘culture.’ I structured Computacenter's Employer Value Proposition into six genuinely distinct, non-overlapping pillars — culture, growth, reward, purpose, flexibility, and leadership — validated against competitive benchmarking and employee insight data, so every piece of employer messaging could be traced to exactly one pillar. No double-counting, no gaps.

Result → Adopted as Computacenter's global employer-branding standard
