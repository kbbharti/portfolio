Employer Branding · Case Study In Progress

The Outsider's 100-Day Plan

If I were hired tomorrow to fix employer branding at a company I've never worked for.

What It Is

Choose one real, publicly known company with a visibly inconsistent employer brand — using only public Glassdoor reviews, its careers page, and its LinkedIn presence as raw material — and write the 100-day plan I'd bring as their new Head of Employer Brand: diagnosis, quick wins, and a pillar-based framework, built entirely from public information.

Why I'm Building It

Employer branding work usually happens entirely inside a company's own walls, using data I'd never get to see from outside. I wanted to test whether the same diagnostic process still holds up with nothing but what's publicly visible — reviews, careers pages, LinkedIn — on a company I have no relationship with at all.

How I'm Approaching It

  1. Choose a company with visibly public gaps — inconsistent Glassdoor themes, a stale careers page, no clear culture narrative. Pick one to critique constructively, not to embarrass.
  2. Gather only public signals: reviews, job postings, LinkedIn presence, recent press — no claims of insider knowledge.
  3. Diagnose the gap using an EVP-style framework, and write a 100-day plan: first 30 days (diagnosis), next 30 (quick wins), final 40 (structural fixes).
  4. Frame it explicitly as an independent thought exercise — the credibility comes from the rigor of the public-data analysis, not from pretending to have access that doesn't exist.
Note to self

State clearly, in the piece itself, that this is an outside analysis based on public information only. That framing is what makes it a credible show of skill rather than an overreaching claim.
