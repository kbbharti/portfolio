Strategic Analysis In Progress

The Strategy Postmortem Series

Reverse-engineering real corporate decisions to sharpen judgment in public.

What It Is

An ongoing series where I pick one real, publicly documented corporate strategy decision — a rebrand, a pivot, a product launch, a leadership announcement — and write a structured postmortem: what likely drove the decision, what worked, and what I would have done differently, using the same frameworks (MECE, Pyramid Principle) I use at work.

Why I'm Building It

I kept noticing that most reactions to a company's rebrand, pivot, or layoff announcement were just reactions — a hot take, not an analysis. I wanted a discipline that forced me to look at the actual public facts before forming an opinion, structure that opinion properly, and write it down somewhere I could be held to it later.

How I'm Approaching It

  1. Pick one real, publicly documented strategic decision per month — a rebrand, a widely covered pivot, a leadership memo that made news.
  2. Research only public facts: earnings calls, press coverage, leadership statements. No speculation presented as fact.
  3. Write a structured postmortem: Context → The Decision → What Likely Drove It → What Worked → What I'd Have Done Differently → Framework Used.
  4. Publish on LinkedIn or the portfolio blog, building a visible, growing trail of judgment over time rather than a single one-off piece.
Note to self

Pick decisions to critique constructively, not to score points off a company having a bad quarter — the goal is demonstrated judgment, not cheap contrarianism. A postmortem of a decision that worked, explaining why, is just as valuable as critiquing one that didn't.
