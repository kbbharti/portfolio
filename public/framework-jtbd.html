How I Think — Model 01

Jobs-to-be-Done

What is the audience actually ‘hiring’ a product or program to do — before touching messaging.

What It Is

Jobs-to-be-Done reframes a product or program not as a bundle of features, but as something an audience ‘hires’ to make progress on a specific job in their life or work. The question isn't ‘what does this do’ — it's ‘what job is someone trying to get done, and does this actually help them do it.’

Why It Matters

Most marketing underperforms not because the message is unclear, but because it's answering a question the audience never asked. A rewards program pitched as ‘earn more rewards’ fails if the seller's real job is ‘reduce uncertainty about which actions actually pay off.’ Reframing around the real job changes what the audience hears.

How I Apply It

  1. Map the audience's actual goal in their own words, not the product's feature list.
  2. Identify what ‘hiring’ the product replaces — a manual process, a competitor tool, or doing nothing at all.
  3. Build messaging around the job, not the feature.
  4. Test whether adoption follows the reframed job, not just awareness of the program.

In Practice — Seller Rewards Program

Amazon's Seller Rewards Program had low awareness among SMB sellers — not because they didn't know it existed, but because it didn't map to a job they were trying to get done. I mapped friction across the onboarding journey and reframed the program around the seller's actual job — reducing uncertainty about which actions paid off — rather than ‘earn rewards.’ Segmented messaging built around that reframing is what moved adoption, not a louder awareness push.

Result → 21% increase in seller acquisition, 11% dormant seller reactivation
